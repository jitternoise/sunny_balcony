Flash Flood — Breakable & Delayed Tiles

Design brainstorm: block/terrain types that change state after N ticks — some break down, one builds up. Companion to icon-system.md.

Tile catalog

8 concepts — 7 break down, 1 (the Beaver Dam) builds up.
Sandbag Wall
Timer
Behaves exactly like a Wall — fully blocks and backs up water — but crumbles to open terrain after a fixed number of ticks, whether or not water ever touched it. Teaches the whole mechanic: "hold the line just long enough," not permanent redirection.
Suggested fuse: 4–6 ticks · breaks into EMPTY
Ice Dam
Timer
Same idea as the Sandbag Wall but a much shorter fuse — themed as melting rather than crumbling. Good as the very first breakable tile a player meets: it fails fast enough to be self-explanatory in one attempt.
Suggested fuse: 2–3 ticks · breaks into EMPTY
Rope Bridge Diverter
Timer
A Diverter-Left/Right that only redirects for a set number of ticks, then goes slack and lets water fall straight through again. The interesting part isn't whether a block exists — it's that the flow direction itself changes mid-level.
Suggested fuse: 3–5 ticks · breaks into natural fall
Mud Levee
Contact
Only counts down on ticks where water is actually pressing against it — it doesn't decay sitting idle. Rewards players who reroute flow away from it early to preserve it longer, and punishes brute-forcing water straight at it.
Suggested threshold: 3–4 contact ticks · breaks into EMPTY
Reed Debris Catch
Contact
Acts like a Splitter, but clogs a little more with every pass. Once it hits its contact threshold it bursts, releasing every bit of water it had been diverting as one sudden surge in a single direction — a way to create a delayed second wave without a second source.
Suggested threshold: 3 contact ticks · breaks into surge + open fork
Sandbag Row (Chain Levee)
Chain
Several adjacent breakable walls, linked: if one breaks early from overload, it shaves ticks off its neighbors' timers too, simulating a levee failure cascading sideways. Best saved for a climactic level rather than an early teaching moment.
Suggested fuse: 5 ticks each, –2 to neighbors on break
Sandcastle Town Shield
Timer
A temporary answer to "should towns get their own block?" — protects a town cell from instant-loss, but only for a fixed number of ticks. Forces the player to have a permanent redirect solution in place before the shield expires rather than relying on it alone.
Suggested fuse: 6–8 ticks · breaks into exposed Town
Beaver Dam
Grows — Contact
The mirror of every other tile here: placed dormant — water flows straight through it untouched — and only becomes active once it finishes "building." Once complete it behaves like a Diverter, permanently redirecting water to one side. Builds only on ticks where water is actually flowing over the cell (a beaver needs the current to work with), so — like the Mud Levee — routing water away from it stalls its progress.
Suggested build time: 4 contact ticks · becomes: Diverter-Left/Right
Implementation sketch. This maps onto the existing BlockData/TickBehavior pattern with two new exported fields — breaks_after_ticks: int and break_into: TickBehavior (or a flag meaning "revert to EMPTY") — plus a per-cell counter dictionary in HexBoard.gd, the same pattern already used for active_geysers. Timer-based and contact-based tiles are the same system with a different increment rule: timer tiles increment every tick(), contact tiles increment only on ticks where _try_enter() lands on that cell. Chain tiles just need each break to look up its placed neighbors and dock their counters. Visual feedback (crack overlays like the explainer above) can key off ticks_remaining / breaks_after_ticks to show 2–3 wear stages without new art per tile.

Beaver Dam is the same system inverted: appears_after_ticks: int and appear_into: TickBehavior (here, a Diverter). Before the counter hits its target the cell behaves as plain EMPTY in the simulation; once it hits the target, _resolve_block_targets() starts treating that cell as if a Diverter block were placed there. Since it's contact-based like the Mud Levee, it shares the same "only increments on _try_enter()" hook — no new counter machinery needed, just a second dictionary (growing_dams, alongside active_geysers) and one more branch in the cell-behavior lookup.
