Flash Flood — Icon & Terrain System

Flat-vector glyphs designed to read at small mobile size and stay legible inside a pointy-top hex silhouette. Each block/terrain type gets one unmistakable shape and one dedicated color — never rely on color alone, since a player scanning fast should be able to tell blocks apart from silhouette even before the color registers. Bold dark outline (#1e2530-ish) on every glyph keeps them grounded against any hex fill.

Block types (player-placed)

Wall
A boulder reads as "solid" at a glance. Only block that stops natural fall.
Diverter Right
Chevron points the exit direction directly — no legend needed.
Diverter Left
Exact mirror of Diverter-Right — same color, opposite silhouette, so the pair reads as a family.
Splitter
One stem, two arrows — visually explains "sends water both ways" without a tooltip.

Terrain & hazards (fixed per level)

Fire
Warm gradient flame on a dark ember tile — the hottest-looking cell on the board, on purpose.
Pool
Basin + 4 corner tabs mirror the in-HUD 4-box bar, so the cell itself previews its own win condition.
Town
Earthy brown, house glyph. Deliberately the "calmest"-looking hazard — the danger is what it means, not how it looks, so the loss sting has to carry the weight instead of the tile.
Water Source
Converging river strands flowing into a baked-in down-left arrow — teaches the "first move is always down-left" rule on sight, every level, no text needed.
Blocked / Unplayable
Neutral gray hatch, no hazard color — reads as "not part of the puzzle," distinct from every danger tile.

Rule of thumb applied throughout: block types (things the player places) sit in the cool blue/violet/gray family; terrain hazards and rewards (things already on the board) sit in warm/earth tones except Pool, which stays blue because it's water-family, not danger-family. That single hue split — "cool = tool, warm = consequence" — is the fastest way for a new player's eye to sort a busy board without reading anything.

How it looks on an actual board

Even with zero labels, this board reads: water enters top-left, a Diverter-Right sits one row down to fight the leftward drift, a Splitter and a Wall shape the fire's approach, and the town and pool sit far enough apart that a misread of one doesn't cost the other.
