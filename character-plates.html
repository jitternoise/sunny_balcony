Flash Flood — art direction, first pass

Field guide to the mountain

Fifteen characters for a game with no words in it. Every one is drawn in the existing icon system — flat fill, one navy outline, 100×100 viewBox — so it drops straight onto a board cell beside the wall, pool and splitter glyphs already in the project. Shown here on the hex tile they'll actually live in.

The two birds

Same construction, mirrored and desaturated. That mirroring is the story: she always faces uphill.

Player character

The bird

The only animal that can travel the whole river in a day. Goes out ahead of the flood and sets the stones. White bib is the only pure white on the board, so she always reads.

Always upstream

The other bird

Same species, duller, larger, and mirrored — she always faces uphill. Her staging rule is the whole characterisation: she never appears below the plant until the final frame.

One animal per chapter

Mapped onto the thirteen level groups that already exist. No level data changes.

One ant carrying one drop. A colony reads as a crowd; a single ant reads as an ant.

Ch 9 Levels 55–62

Ch 10 Levels 63–70

Geyser Country

Hot springs. The one animal that is genuinely at home in strange water.

Ch 11 Levels 71–78

Face stripes do the work. Body stays neutral grey so the head reads first.

Ch 12 Levels 79–86

Not one animal — a crowd glyph. Three depths, three silhouettes, gaps between them.

Ch 13 Levels 87–100

The Gauntlet

The dry lake at the bottom. Last chapter, biggest boards, longest wait.

The pool bar is the story

The four-box status bar above a pool, replaced with the animal waiting at it. Same data, same four beats, no new simulation state — and every pool in all 100 levels becomes a small silent scene.

Beats of connected water — cumulative, so the animal never walks backwards.

Does it read at tile size?

58 px, roughly what a cell measures on a phone. Anything that turns to mush here needs redrawing before it ships.

House style

viewBox="0 0 100 100"

Matches every glyph in assets/icons/. Godot imports SVG natively.

stroke #0b3d63 · width 5

One outline colour for the whole game, round joins and caps, no gradients.

flat fill, no shading

Silhouette first. If it fails as a solid shape it fails on a phone.

First-pass concepts, not final art. The sheepdog, the crowd glyph and the mole are the three weakest reads and should be redrawn before anyone animates them. Nothing here is wired into the engine yet — the pool-bar sequence is the change worth making first.
